EV Charging Strategies for Solar Homes
======================================

Electric vehicles are the largest flexible load in a modern household.
A 40 kWh daily top-up at an on-peak tariff of ₹12.40/kWh costs ₹496.
The same energy at an off-peak tariff of ₹5.50 costs ₹220. If half of
that energy is supplied by rooftop solar that would otherwise be exported
at a low feed-in rate, the cash saving is even larger.

Preferred charging order
1. Midday solar surplus (typically 10:00–15:00). Cap charge power so the
   house still covers HVAC and other daytime loads; leftover PV goes to
   the car.
2. Overnight off-peak (23:00–06:00). Use a scheduled charge in the car
   or wallbox. This is the default when the next-day forecast is cloudy
   or the car must be ready before sunrise.
3. Never charge on a timer that starts at 18:00–22:00 unless the battery
   is critically low. That window is both expensive and solar-empty.

Practical settings
- Target a 20–80% daily window rather than 0–100%. Cycle life improves
  and you keep a buffer for an unplanned evening trip.
- If the wallbox supports solar-following (or EcoHome can dispatch it),
  enable it on weekdays you work from home.
- Preconditioning the cabin while plugged in uses grid/solar energy
  instead of the traction battery and can be timed for off-peak.

Safety and grid etiquette
- Prefer 3.3 kW (16 A single-phase) scheduled charging over an
  opportunistic 7 kW blast during peak, unless solar surplus is high.
- On a feeder with many EVs, staggering start minutes (e.g. 23:12 rather
  than 23:00) reduces local voltage dip.

Worked example
Charge 18 kWh. On-peak cost = 18 × 12.40 = ₹223. Off-peak cost =
18 × 5.50 = ₹99. Solar-covered 10 kWh + 8 kWh off-peak ≈ ₹44. Daily
saving versus naïve evening charging: ₹124–₹179, or ₹3,700–₹5,400/month
for a daily commuter.
