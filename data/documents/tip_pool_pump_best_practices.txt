Pool Pump Best Practices
========================

A single-speed pool pump is often the third-largest load in a home
that has a pool, quietly drawing 1–2 kW for 8–12 hours. It is also
one of the most flexible loads: water does not care whether it was
filtered at 11:00 or at 21:00.

Scheduling
- Default run window: the solar plateau, 10:00–16:00.
- If the forecast is heavily overcast, slide the run into off-peak
  overnight rather than parking it on the evening peak.
- Avoid 18:00–23:00 unless water quality has slipped and you need an
  emergency extra cycle.

Runtime, not just clock time
Most residential pools stay clean on 6–8 hours a day in cool weather
and 8–10 in hot weather. Running 12 hours "because that is how the
builder left it" is common and wasteful. Cut runtime first, then
shift what remains under solar.

Variable-speed upgrades
A VSD pump at 1,200–1,800 RPM can match the turnover of a
single-speed pump at a third of the energy. Payback is often 18–36
months at TOU tariffs. If EcoHome is asked for ROI on a pump
replacement, use that band and the household's actual daily hours.

Maintenance that saves kWh
- A clogged skimmer or dirty filter raises head pressure and energy
  use. Check weekly in dusty / leaf-heavy seasons.
- Leaking valves that force the pump to work against a closed path
  are an easy miss.

Safety
Never disable a pump that is also driving a solar water heater loop
or a required sanitizer system without confirming the linked system
can idle. Optimization stops at the point where water quality or
equipment warranty is at risk.
