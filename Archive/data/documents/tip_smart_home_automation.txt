Smart Home Automation Tips
==========================

Automation is useful when it removes a daily decision. It is wasteful
when it adds always-on hubs and gadgets that sip 5–15 W forever.

High-ROI automations
- EV wallbox schedule. The single best automation in a solar home.
  Default: start under the solar plateau; fallback to 23:00 off-peak.
- Dishwasher and washer delay-start. One button, every cycle.
- Storage geyser timer. Heat at midday, off overnight unless a morning
  boost is actually needed.
- Occupancy-based lighting and plug strips in the office and TV unit.
- HVAC scenes: "away" (+2°C), "pre-cool" (solar window), "peak coast".

Rules that work without a fancy platform
1. If irradiance > 500 W/m² and the tariff is not on-peak, release
   flexible loads (EV, pump, geyser).
2. If the tariff flips to on-peak, pause flexible loads and hold HVAC.
3. If the forecast for tomorrow is overcast, pre-charge the EV tonight.

What not to automate
- Cooking. People eat when they eat.
- Medical devices and the refrigerator.
- Security lighting that needs to be predictable.

Standby tax
A house with ten "smart" bulbs, two speakers, a streaming stick and a
hub can waste 30–60 kWh/month on standby. Audit plugs once a quarter.
If an automation needs a cloud round-trip to turn off a lamp, prefer a
local timer.

EcoHome can be the brain even when the house is dumb: it emits a
schedule you load into the wallbox and the appliance delay-start. You
do not need a full home bus to capture most of the saving.
