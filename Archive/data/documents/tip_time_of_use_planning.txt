Time-of-Use Planning
====================

A TOU tariff is a price signal. The EcoHome advisor reads it the same
way a trader reads a market: buy (import) when the price is low or
when on-site generation makes the *net* price low; avoid importing
when the price is high and solar is gone.

Typical residential TOU shape used by this project
- Off-peak  23:00–06:00   cheapest import, no solar
- Shoulder  06:00–10:00 and 16:00–18:00
- Solar window 10:00–16:00  mid-rate but high PV, so *net* cost is low
- On-peak   18:00–23:00   most expensive, zero PV

Weekend rates are often discounted. If they are, dump deferrable work
(laundry marathon, EV "catch-up" charge, pool extra filtration) onto
Saturday late night / Sunday morning.

How to plan a day
1. Pull the tariff table and mark on-peak hours as red.
2. Pull the weather / irradiance forecast and mark high-PV hours as green.
3. Place flexible loads on green first, leftover red-avoiding hours
   second (overnight off-peak).
4. Leave HVAC and cooking where life needs them, but pre-condition
   thermally so the expensive hours are coasting hours.

Worked dishwasher example
A 1.4 kWh eco cycle.
- On-peak 18:30 start: 1.4 × 12.40 = ₹17.36
- Off-peak 23:30 start: 1.4 × 5.50 = ₹7.70
- Midday solar (net import ≈ 0.2 kWh): ≈ ₹1.60
Saving versus the naïve evening start: ₹10–₹16 per run. At one run a
day that is ₹300–₹480/month from a single appliance.

Demand charges
Some commercial and a few residential tariffs add a per-kW peak demand
charge. In that world, never start the EV, water heater and dryer in
the same 15-minute interval. EcoHome's scheduler staggers them.
