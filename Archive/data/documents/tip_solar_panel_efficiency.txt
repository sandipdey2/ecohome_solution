Rooftop Solar Efficiency and Self-Consumption
=============================================

A 5 kW array in Bengaluru, installed with a realistic 0.76–0.80
performance ratio, produces roughly 18–24 kWh on a clear dry-season day
and 8–14 kWh on a monsoon overcast day. Annual specific yield for a
well-sited south-facing roof is typically 1,400–1,600 kWh/kWp.

What actually moves the needle
- Soiling: Bengaluru dust and construction grit can cut yield 8–15%
  between cleanings. A monthly rinse in the dry season is high-ROI.
- Temperature: Module efficiency drops ~0.4%/°C above 25°C. Roof-mount
  standoffs that allow airflow under the array beat flush mounts.
- Shade: One shaded cell in a poorly designed string can kneecap a
  whole channel. Module-level power electronics help if trees or a
  water tank throw a moving shadow.
- Inverter clipping: A 5 kW array on a 4 kW inverter will clip on the
  best hours. That is acceptable if those hours are rare; it is not
  acceptable if you sized the inverter too small for a wide roof.

Self-consumption versus export
Retail import tariffs are several times typical net-metering or
gross-metering export credits. Using a midday kWh in the house is
almost always more valuable than exporting it. The EcoHome advisor
therefore treats "shift load under the solar curve" as the default
strategy and "export and re-import later" as the fallback.

Forecasting
Cloud cover and irradiance drive the next-day plan. A forecast that
calls for <200 W/m² through the solar window means: do not count on
PV for the EV; schedule the car overnight instead. A forecast of
700–900 W/m² means: park the flexible loads under the 11:00–14:00 peak.
