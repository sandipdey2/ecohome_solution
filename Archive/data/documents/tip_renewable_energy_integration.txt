Renewable Energy Integration
============================

Rooftop solar only cuts the bill if the house actually uses the electrons.
Integration means matching load shape to generation shape, then deciding
what to do with the leftovers.

Self-consumption hierarchy
1. Serve must-run loads (fridge, standby, daytime HVAC).
2. Pull flexible loads under the solar curve (EV, pump, geyser, laundry).
3. Store the remainder in a battery if you have one.
4. Export what is left. Export credits are almost always worse than
   avoided retail import.

Sizing sanity
A 5 kW array on a Bengaluru roof yields roughly 18–24 kWh on a clear
day and 8–14 kWh in the monsoon. If daily flexible load is 20 kWh of
EV charging, the array cannot cover it every day — overnight off-peak
is the planned overflow, not a failure.

Inverter and export limits
Some feeders cap export. When that happens, self-consumption is not
just cheaper, it is the only way to keep producing. EcoHome should
prefer on-site use even more aggressively in export-limited homes.

Other renewables
A small balcony wind turbine in a dense city is almost never worth the
structural hassle. A solar water heater (thermal, not PV) is the
forgotten renewable and pairs beautifully with a PV-timed geyser that
now only trims the tank.

Grid interaction
Net metering, gross metering and behind-the-meter tariffs change the
math. The advisor assumes retail import is expensive and export is
cheap. If your utility flips that, say so in the home profile.
