Energy Storage Optimization
===========================

A home battery is a time machine for electrons. It is not a magic
bill-shrinker. Charge it with cheap or solar energy; discharge it into
expensive evening hours. Anything else wastes cycle life.

When a battery pays
- Wide TOU spread (on-peak much more expensive than off-peak or solar).
- Poor export credit, so midday surplus would otherwise be given away.
- Frequent outages where avoided diesel / lost work has a cash value.

When it does not
- Flat tariffs and generous net metering.
- Tiny arrays that never produce a surplus after the house load.
- Oversized batteries that sit at 50% forever. Unused capacity still
  ages.

Dispatch rules EcoHome should follow
1. Charge from PV after must-run house load is met, up to a 90–95% cap.
2. Hold the battery through the afternoon unless an outage is forecast.
3. Discharge into 18:00–23:00 on-peak, covering HVAC and cooking.
4. Reserve 20–30% state of charge for an evening grid blink.
5. Do not charge from on-peak grid. Do not discharge into off-peak
   just to "use the battery".

Pairing with an EV
The traction battery is also storage, but it leaves the driveway.
Never flatten the home battery to charge the car at 21:00; charge the
car at noon instead. Use the home battery for the immovable evening
peak.

Cycle life
Most LFP packs are happiest between 20 and 90%. Daily 0–100% cycles
for a few rupees of extra arbitrage are a bad trade. EcoHome should
optimise net cost *including* a small wear cost per kWh cycled
(typically ₹0.5–1.5/kWh-cycled depending on pack price and warranty).

ROI sketch
A 5 kWh usable pack that shifts 4 kWh/day from ₹12.40 to solar-equivalent
₹0–₹5.50 saves roughly ₹25–₹50/day. Capex recovery is measured in
years, not months. Be honest about that when a user asks.
