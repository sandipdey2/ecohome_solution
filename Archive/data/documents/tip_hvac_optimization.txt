HVAC and Thermostat Optimization
================================

In a warm climate the air-conditioner is usually the #1 or #2 annual
kWh consumer, second only to an EV that charges at home. Unlike an EV,
HVAC is only partly flexible — people want the room comfortable when
they are in it — but thermal mass still lets you move a useful slice
of compressor runtime.

Set-point discipline
- Each extra degree of cooling costs roughly 6–8% more energy on a
  typical split AC. 26°C with ceiling fans is the comfort/cost sweet
  spot for most Indian households; 22–23°C is an expensive habit.
- Use "dry" / dehumidify mode on muggy monsoon afternoons rather than
  slashing the set-point. Latent load is the real enemy then.

Pre-cooling before the tariff peak
If 18:00–23:00 is on-peak, drop the set-point by 1°C between 15:00 and
17:30 (solar still helping, tariff not yet peaked) and let the building
coast. The compressor then cycles less during the expensive window.
This works best in apartments with some thermal mass and closed
curtains; it works poorly in a west-facing glass box.

Maintenance
- Clogged filters can add 10–15% to runtime. Clean monthly in the dusty
  season.
- Outdoor coil fins packed with lint and leaves do the same. A yearly
  hose-down is cheap insurance.
- A 1.5-ton unit serving a room that a 1-ton unit could handle will
  short-cycle and waste energy. Right-sizing matters more than brand.

When not to "optimize"
Do not cycle the AC off for an hour in the peak if the alternative is
cranking it to 20°C afterwards. Recovery spikes can erase the saving
and wreck comfort. Prefer a 1°C set-point change over hard on/off.
